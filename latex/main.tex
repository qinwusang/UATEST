\title{SNR-Mismatched SwinJSCC: Uncertainty-Aware Deep JSCC Under Imperfect SNR Estimation}

\author{Anonymous Authors}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Adaptive Deep JSCC image transmission conditions neural encoders and decoders on channel SNR, but standard SwinJSCC evaluation assumes that the modulation SNR equals the true physical SNR. This letter studies SNR-mismatched SwinJSCC under imperfect SNR estimation by separating the physical SNR from the estimated modulation SNR. Perfect-SNR fine-tuning improves off-diagonal PSNR by only 0.03 dB, whereas uncertainty-aware training with $\Delta=3$ dB improves it by 1.92 dB with 0.09 dB matched-SNR loss. Wider perturbations reveal a robustness--fidelity trade-off.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Deep joint source-channel coding, imperfect SNR estimation, semantic communications, SNR mismatch, SwinJSCC.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{D}{eep} joint source-channel coding (Deep JSCC) directly maps source samples to channel symbols and can avoid the sharp threshold behavior of separate source and channel coding under channel variation \cite{shannon1948mathematical,cover2006elements,bourtsoulatze2019deepjscc}. For wireless image transmission, recent Deep JSCC systems have incorporated feedback, bandwidth agility, OFDM transmission, attention modules, and nonlinear transform coding \cite{kurka2020deepjsccf,kurka2021bandwidth,yang2021ofdm,xu2022attention,dai2022ntscc}. These developments are closely related to semantic communications, where the recovered signal quality is optimized directly rather than through bit-level recovery alone \cite{xie2020initial,xie2021semantic}.

SwinJSCC extends adaptive Deep JSCC by combining a Swin Transformer backbone with modulation networks that condition latent features on the channel SNR and bandwidth ratio \cite{liu2021swin,yang2024swinjscc}. Existing evaluation, however, normally follows a perfect-SNR convention: the SNR used by Channel ModNet is the same value that determines physical channel noise. In an AWGN setting, the practical uncertainty is more precisely an SNR estimation mismatch. The estimated SNR may be biased by pilot noise, delayed feedback, or prediction error, causing the modulation network to allocate features for a different channel quality than the transmitted symbols actually experience.

This letter studies this SNR-mismatched setting. The model architecture is unchanged; only the SNR interface is separated into the true physical SNR $\gamma$ and the estimated modulation SNR $\hat{\gamma}$. The contribution is not a new Transformer backbone, but a training and evaluation protocol for imperfect SNR estimation. We formulate the corresponding risk, introduce uncertainty-aware (UA) training with bounded SNR perturbations, and compare it with perfect-SNR fine-tuning, wider perturbations, independent random SNR conditioning, and a Rayleigh-channel check.

\section{System Model and Problem Formulation}

Let $\mathbf{x}\in[0,1]^{3\times H\times W}$ denote an input image and $C$ denote the bottleneck dimension. The encoder produces
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z}=F_{\theta}(\mathbf{x},\hat{\gamma},C),
\label{eq:encoder}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\gamma}$ is the estimated SNR supplied to Channel ModNet. Adjacent real latent components are paired into complex channel symbols, $s_k=z_{2k-1}+jz_{2k}$. The implementation normalizes these symbols as
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{s}=\frac{\mathbf{z}_{\rm pair}}{\sqrt{2\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{z}^{2}]}},
\label{eq:power_norm}
\end{equation}
so that two real components form one unit-power complex symbol on average.

For an AWGN channel with true SNR $\gamma$, the channel output is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{y}=T_{\gamma}(\mathbf{s})=\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{n},
\label{eq:awgn}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{n}_{\rm R}+j\mathbf{n}_{\rm I}$ and
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{n}_{\rm R},\mathbf{n}_{\rm I}\sim
\mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\frac{1}{2\cdot10^{\gamma/10}}\mathbf{I}\right).
\label{eq:noise}
\end{equation}
The total complex noise power is therefore $1/10^{\gamma/10}$. The decoder reconstructs
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{x}}=G_{\theta}(\mathbf{y},\hat{\gamma}).
\label{eq:decoder}
\end{equation}

The perfect-SNR assumption is
\begin{equation}
\hat{\gamma}=\gamma,
\label{eq:perfect_snr}
\end{equation}
which corresponds to the risk
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{R}_{\rm p}(\theta)=
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x},\gamma}
\!\left[
d\!\left(G_{\theta}(T_{\gamma}(F_{\theta}(\mathbf{x},\gamma,C)),\gamma),\mathbf{x}\right)
\right].
\label{eq:risk_perfect}
\end{equation}
With SNR estimation error,
\begin{equation}
\hat{\gamma}=\mathrm{clip}(\gamma+e,\gamma_{\min},\gamma_{\max}),
\label{eq:mismatch_model}
\end{equation}
and the deployment risk becomes
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{R}_{\rm m}(\theta)=
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x},\gamma,e}
\!\left[
d\!\left(G_{\theta}(T_{\gamma}(F_{\theta}(\mathbf{x},\hat{\gamma},C)),\hat{\gamma}),\mathbf{x}\right)
\right].
\label{eq:risk_mismatch}
\end{equation}
Thus \eqref{eq:risk_perfect} is the special case of \eqref{eq:risk_mismatch} with $e=0$.

\section{SNR-Mismatch Training}

UA training samples the true SNR and estimated-SNR error as
\begin{equation}
\gamma\sim\mathcal{S},\quad
e\sim\mathcal{U}[-\Delta,\Delta],\quad
\hat{\gamma}=\mathrm{clip}(\gamma+e,\gamma_{\min},\gamma_{\max}).
\label{eq:ua_sampling}
\end{equation}
For a mini-batch $\mathcal{B}$, the empirical objective is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm UA}(\theta)=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|}
\sum_{\mathbf{x}_i\in\mathcal{B}}
d\!\left(G_{\theta}(T_{\gamma_i}(F_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_i,\hat{\gamma}_i,C)),\hat{\gamma}_i),\mathbf{x}_i\right).
\label{eq:ua_risk}
\end{equation}
When $\Delta=0$, this reduces to perfect-SNR fine-tuning. When $\Delta>0$, the training distribution covers a neighborhood of plausible SNR estimates and therefore optimizes an empirical approximation of the mismatch risk in \eqref{eq:risk_mismatch}, rather than the degenerate perfect-SNR risk in \eqref{eq:risk_perfect}.

The implementation also supports a robust auxiliary loss using $K$ estimated SNR samples for the same true SNR. Let
\begin{equation}
\ell_k=d\!\left(G_{\theta}(T_{\gamma}(F_{\theta}(\mathbf{x},\hat{\gamma}_k,C)),\hat{\gamma}_k),\mathbf{x}\right),
\quad k=1,\ldots,K .
\label{eq:sample_loss}
\end{equation}
The robust loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\rm rob}
=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\ell_k
+\lambda_{\rm tail}\,\mathrm{Top}_{1-\alpha}(\{\ell_k\}_{k=1}^{K})
+\lambda_{\rm cons}\frac{1}{K-1}\sum_{k=2}^{K}\|\hat{\mathbf{x}}_k-\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1\|_1 ,
\label{eq:robust_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{Top}_{1-\alpha}(\cdot)$ averages the largest $\lceil K(1-\alpha)\rceil$ losses. This auxiliary form is used only as a robustness check in this letter; the central comparison is the effect of the SNR-error width $\Delta$.

If the end-to-end reconstruction map and distortion are locally Lipschitz with respect to $e$, then for some constant $K_{\theta}$,
\begin{equation}
\left|d(H_{\theta}(\mathbf{x},\gamma,e),\mathbf{x})
-d(H_{\theta}(\mathbf{x},\gamma,0),\mathbf{x})\right|
\le K_{\theta}|e|.
\label{eq:lipschitz}
\end{equation}
This observation motivates training over an error neighborhood but does not imply pointwise dominance at every SNR pair.

\section{Experimental Results}

The main evaluation uses CIFAR10 over AWGN with $C=32$ and $\mathcal{S}=\{1,4,7,10,13\}$ dB. All fine-tuned models initialize from the original SwinJSCC \texttt{w/SA} CIFAR10/AWGN/C32 checkpoint and use Adam with learning rate $10^{-4}$ and batch size 256. The D0 baseline uses $\Delta=0$ and is included to separate the effect of additional perfect-SNR fine-tuning from mismatch-aware sampling. The reported mean-square error is computed on pixel values scaled to $[0,255]$, i.e.,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MSE}_{255}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|\mathbf{x}_{255}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{255}\|_2^2\right],
\label{eq:mse255}
\end{equation}
and the PSNR is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PSNR}=10\log_{10}\frac{255^2}{\mathrm{MSE}_{255}}.
\label{eq:psnr}
\end{equation}
MS-SSIM(dB) is computed as
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MS\text{-}SSIM}_{\rm dB}
=-10\log_{10}(1-\mathrm{MS\text{-}SSIM}).
\label{eq:msssimdb}
\end{equation}
For a mismatch matrix $\mathbf{P}_{i,j}=\mathrm{PSNR}(\gamma_i,\hat{\gamma}_j)$, the off-diagonal average is
\begin{equation}
\overline{P}_{\rm off}=
\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\sum_{i\ne j}\mathbf{P}_{i,j}.
\label{eq:offdiag}
\end{equation}

The auxiliary variants in Table~\ref{tab:auxiliary} show that tail and consistency terms provide additional tail-oriented operating points, but they do not replace the simpler D3 result as the central average-robustness setting. For example, Tail+Cons-UA improves worst-case PSNR by \TailConsWorstGain{} dB, and the stronger tail setting reaches a \TailLtOneWorstGain{} dB worst-case gain. This result is reported as an additional channel check, not as a comprehensive fading-channel study.

\section{Conclusion}

This letter revisits adaptive SwinJSCC under SNR estimation mismatch. By decoupling the true physical SNR from the estimated SNR used by Channel ModNet, UA training aligns the training objective with bounded SNR errors. Perfect-SNR fine-tuning provides negligible off-diagonal improvement, whereas UA-D3 improves off-diagonal PSNR by 1.92 dB with only 0.09 dB matched-SNR loss on CIFAR10/AWGN/C=32. Wider perturbations, random SNR conditioning, and auxiliary robust losses expose controllable robustness--fidelity and average--tail trade-offs. Future work will extend the evaluation to high-resolution images, rate adaptation, and broader fading-channel conditions.
